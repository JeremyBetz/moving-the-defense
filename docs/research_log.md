## 2026-08-28 — Conceptual Refinement After Phase 1C

Phase 1C motivates preserving four distinct constructs: pairwise distance closure, defender absolute approach, collective defensive translation, and defender opponent-directed deviation from collective movement. Pairwise convergence alone does not show whether the defender, attacker, or both produced it. Defender absolute approach alone does not show whether the movement exceeds ordinary block translation.

A counterexample broadens the construct. A defender may remain stationary or retreat while the defensive block retreats faster. Absolute defender–attacker distance may remain stable or increase, yet the defender's residual movement relative to the block can be opponent-directed. In relational terms, the defender has maintained or strengthened the opponent relationship relative to collective movement without literally closing absolute distance.

**Engage** is recorded as a provisional replacement term for **Close**, not finalized terminology. Existing documents and historical Phase 0/1 descriptions are not being globally renamed. The refinement is intended to prevent “Close” from silently collapsing pairwise convergence, defender motion, and defender deviation from collective motion into one construct.

The **Structure → Track → Close → Recover → Structure** state-machine interpretation is now under explicit reconsideration. Current evidence is compatible with Structure, Track, provisional Engage/Close, and Recover being overlapping behavioral dimensions or relational constraints rather than mutually exclusive states. The historical framework remains an organizing hypothesis, not an accepted ontology.

No new metric, state definition, classifier, or notebook was introduced in this documentation update.

## 2026-08-28 — Prior-Art Refinement

### Closest Neighbors Identified

Two 2026 works materially narrow the candidate novelty. Groom et al. infer time-resolved man-marking and zonal assignments and assignment switches with a covariate-dependent HMM for corner kicks. Calero-Sanz et al. construct open-play marking networks and quantify marking load, coordination, target similarity, and entropy.

The project therefore cannot claim that defensive assignment transitions are unmodeled, or that nobody has asked what attacking movement forces defenders to do.

### Candidate Novelty Narrowed

The earlier broad position—**defensive adjustment / man-zone transitions**—has been refined toward **open-play responsibility resolution, instantaneous ambiguity, and transition execution/recovery**.

The proposed distinction is the process of maintaining collective structure, assuming opponent responsibility, engaging a threat, and restoring structure while the structural reference itself moves. Assignment switch rate, marking entropy, transition frequency, and displacement are related but not interchangeable constructs.

### First-Paper Target Refined

The primary question is whether open-play tracking can identify transitions and periods of ambiguity between collective structure and individual threats. The secondary question is whether attacking movements, including incomplete or non-receiving movements, precede those transitions. Predicting subsequent instability beyond simple displacement is optional later work.

### Research Interpretation

Discovering close prior work is a productive refinement, not a project failure. It removes claims already supported elsewhere, clarifies the necessary comparisons, and makes the empirical construct-validation standard more demanding. The project remains worth investigating, but novelty is provisional until full-text comparison and initial validation show that the proposed ambiguity and recovery processes are distinct and measurable.

## 2026-08-28 — Project Charter Consolidation

### Change in Conceptual Center

The project began with off-ball attacking influence through defensive adjustment. Its primary phenomenon is now stated more precisely as **defensive state change and decision instability**.

Space creation, player gravity, off-ball value, and defensive player evaluation remain possible downstream applications. They are not the initial empirical target.

### Broader Theory and Empirical Limit

The motivating theory is that teams “ask questions” through credible positions, movements, rotations, and threats. A team may gain control by imposing repeated defensive adjustment at comparatively low attacking cost.

Tracking data observes behavior, not cognition, instructions, communication, or mental bandwidth. Empirical claims must therefore concern movement consistent with changes in defensive responsibility, not what players thought.

### Behavioral Framework Retained and Clarified

Four hypothesized regimes remain:

1. Structure
2. Track
3. Close
4. Recover

The cycle **Structure → Track → Close → Recover → Structure** is shorthand only. Transitions can skip, reverse, or involve handoffs. Ambiguous and unexplained observations must be permitted.

The central reference-frame prompt remains: **Which coordinate system makes the defender look most stationary?** Structure and Track concern relative stability; Close and Recover concern directed changes in threat or structural relationships.

### Quantities Kept Separate

The project will not collapse the following into a final metric without empirical justification:

- current behavioral explanation,
- transition frequency,
- state ambiguity,
- persistence,
- displacement,
- recovery time or burden,
- multi-defender coordination,
- downstream spatial or possession consequences.

### Competing Hypothesis Added

Frequent transitions may be handled fluidly by strong defenses. Transition frequency alone must not be treated as harmful. Future analysis must distinguish transition volume from execution quality and consider simpler contextual explanations.

### First-Paper Scope

A defensible first paper would ask whether open-play tracking data can identify shifts between collective structural and individual opponent responsibility, and which attacking movements are associated with those shifts. It need not produce a universal player rating, final gravity metric, causal estimate, or complete theory of game control.

### Methodological Decisions

- Begin with transparent geometry, visualization, and descriptive statistics.
- Do not use subjective tactical labels as unquestioned training truth.
- Validate mathematically defined constructs with a small, stratified set of clear and ambiguous examples.
- Explain every proposed method in soccer terms and then mathematically, including assumptions and failure modes.
- Choose one dataset and one match when data exploration begins.
- Do not select a dataset, algorithm, or final metric during this documentation pass.

### Immediate Next Decision

Before implementation, decide which structural reference candidates should be compared in a transparent Phase 0/1 experiment and what observable evidence would count for or against each candidate.
