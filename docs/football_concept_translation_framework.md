# Football-Concept Translation Framework

## 1. Motivation

The post–Phase 4 research program has one formal primary research question:

> **How can tracking data measure defensive responses to attacking movement in open-play football?**

The motivating football question is: **When an attacker does not receive the ball, can we measure what they made the defense do?** It supplies intuition, not a second formal primary question or a causal claim.

The downstream translation question is whether football concepts such as pinning, dragging, tracking, covering, passing on, and stretching can eventually be translated into validated, interpretable tracking patterns. This is a later research question nested within the broader program. It does not imply that those concepts already have valid operational measurements.

This framing was developed **after** the Phase 4A protocol was frozen and **after** Phase 4B was executed. It did not motivate, select, or influence the successful held-out focal-departure result.

## 2. Evidentiary constraint

**Defensive response** is the broad empirical umbrella: observable individual or collective defensive behavior occurring in the context of attacking positioning or movement. It is behavioral and geometric. Tracking observes positions and movement, not cognition, attention, tactical responsibility, instruction, intention, decisions, or psychological workload.

Association is not attribution, and attribution is not causation. Empirical writing should prefer “associated with,” “corresponds to,” or “is consistent with” over “forced,” “caused,” or “induced” unless a much stronger design supports the latter.

The central methodological warning is:

> **activity ≠ relative movement ≠ unexpected response ≠ attacker-associated response ≠ attacker-induced response ≠ attacking value**

Phase 3 showed why: generic active-play movement can masquerade as a relational construct.

## 3. Three vocabularies

| Language level | Purpose | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Football language** | Names recognizable tactical ideas | pin, drag, track, cover, pass on/handoff, step out, drop, hold, shift, squeeze, stretch, occupy, overload, recover/rebalance, decoy run |
| **Observable tracking language** | Describes reproducible measurements | position, displacement, velocity, relative position, focal-relative path/focal departure, collective translation, local deformation, opponent coupling, defender–opponent distance, persistence, width/depth change, temporal correspondence |
| **Theoretical language** | Organizes hypotheses above measurements | defensive response, relational reconfiguration, ambiguity, collective accommodation, response propagation, recovery burden, attacker-associated response, off-ball influence |

**Football concept ≠ tracking measurement ≠ theoretical mechanism.** A football term may motivate a hypothesis, but geometry does not establish the football interpretation automatically.

## 4. Candidate football concepts and observable consequences

These are candidate signatures, not operational definitions, labels, or classifiers.

### Pinning

**Football hypothesis:** an attacker's position or movement constrains a defender's ability to leave a location or relationship without conceding another threat or space.

Candidate observations include a defender shifting less than the surrounding block; a persistent defender–attacker relationship; the defender remaining unusually wide, deep, or high relative to collective motion; collective movement while the focal defender holds; and spacing changes elsewhere. A stationary defender is not sufficient evidence. Pinning is important precisely because a candidate effect may appear as **prevented or constrained movement**, not large displacement.

### Dragging

Candidate observations include focal departure from collective motion, temporal or directional correspondence with attacker movement, local defensive deformation, and possible space change elsewhere. Focal departure alone is not dragging.

### Tracking

Candidate observations include sustained defender–opponent movement coupling that persists beyond common team and ball motion. Earlier diagnostics rejected stable attacker-relative Cartesian position as a validated general definition of tracking; that negative result remains binding.

### Cover

Candidate observations include one defender departing while a teammate changes local or team-relative geometry in a complementary way, possibly preserving more collective or local coherence. Geometry does not reveal tactical responsibility or prove that one movement caused the other.

### Handoff / pass on

Candidate observations include a weakening defender-A–opponent relationship, strengthening defender-B–opponent relationship, persistence over a coherent interval, and comparatively stable wider defensive geometry. A one-frame nearest-defender identity change is insufficient.

### Stretch

Candidate observations include attacking position or movement increasing or preserving defensive width/depth, preventing expected compression, or changing spacing elsewhere. These require contextual expectation; width alone is not a tactical interpretation.

### Squeeze / compression

Candidate observations include coordinated defensive contraction relative to context or a threat. Raw convex-hull decrease is insufficient. Prior phases showed that local deformation is broader and membership-sensitive.

### Overload response

Candidate observations include a local attacking configuration corresponding to additional defensive concentration or changed relationships. Simple local player counts are insufficient.

### Recovery / rebalance

Candidate observations include return toward justified collective or contextual relationships after departure or deformation. Return to an arbitrary fixed coordinate is insufficient.

### Decoy influence

A future interpretation might concern defensive response associated with attacking movement despite no direct ball involvement. An unrewarded run is not automatically a decoy.

## 5. Alternative explanations

Every candidate signature must retain ordinary alternatives: ball movement, shared block translation, transition, game phase, role/location, space protection, another attacker, tracking noise or occlusion, substitution/membership, and coincidental co-movement. A changed defender–opponent distance does not establish marking, openness, or responsibility. Local contraction does not establish quality. Limited defender movement may reflect role, instruction, fatigue, or lack of relevant threat rather than pinning.

## 6. Common validation ladder

Future concepts must proceed in order:

1. **Football definition:** specify what the concept means in football.
2. **Observable consequences:** state what tracking should plausibly show.
3. **Competing explanations:** list geometry that could look identical.
4. **Candidate measurement:** only then define quantities.
5. **Negative controls:** test generic active play and alternative references.
6. **Held-out replication:** freeze criteria and validate prospectively.
7. **Football interpretation:** test whether validated geometry corresponds to recognizable behavior, potentially with video or annotation.
8. **Attacker association:** test systematic association with a particular attacker or action.
9. **Attribution:** assess whether the attacker plausibly produced the response rather than merely co-occurring with it.
10. **Value:** test whether the response creates useful attacking consequences.

No level licenses skipping the next.

## 7. Core inference ladder and current status

**physical movement → collective defensive movement → individual/local behavior relative to collective movement → contextual expectation → opponent-information association → tactical defensive-response interpretation → attacker attribution → attacking value**

Opponent-information association is a predictive-information rung only. It does not establish tactical response, attacker induction, attribution, or value.

Phase 4B reaches only the third stage. It supports this claim:

> **Focal-relative path is a reproducible focal-versus-collective geometric primitive with stable activity-context structure in these two sample matches.**

It does not estimate an activity-free effect. Focal-relative path remains positively associated with generic activity ($\rho=0.541$ in Game 1 and $0.462$ in Game 2 for focal absolute path). Common translation cancels, all frozen sensitivities preserve the qualitative result, and temporally misaligned similarly active references yield much larger paths. The latter supports contemporaneous focal-versus-collective geometry beyond movement magnitudes alone; it does not establish tactical coordination, organization, synchronization, responsibility, or attention.

## 8. Relationship to current validated primitives

Focal departure can contribute to hypotheses about dragging, holding, stepping, or covering, but it cannot identify any of them by itself. Collective translation is a baseline, not defensive structure. Local deformation, opponent coupling, distances, persistence, and width/depth are currently descriptive candidates with important selection and context limitations. They must remain separate until independently justified.

## 9. Relational reconfiguration

Relational reconfiguration is retained as one possible intermediate form of defensive response: a candidate description of cases in which defender-team, defender-opponent, ball, and local relationships change coherently over time. It is no longer the single phenomenon the entire project must validate. It remains unvalidated; Phase 4B did not validate it.

## 10. Relationship to prior research

This program builds on substantial precedent in player-to-team-centroid geometry, relative phase, synchronization, collective geometry, marking networks, off-ball space generation, defensive pressure following runs, tactical/action vocabularies, basketball gravity, and counterfactual or ghost defensive trajectories. Recent off-ball reviews and taxonomies also emphasize fragmentation and inconsistent terminology. The possible contribution is not invention of pinning or player-versus-team geometry; it may be a validated, interpretable bridge from football concepts to observable defensive behavior, contextual expectation, and later attacker-associated response. That novelty remains hypothetical.

## 11. Long-term attacker attribution and value

Attacker association requires a validated defensive response and a prospectively justified attacker/action link. Attribution requires credible alternatives and expected behavior. Value additionally requires downstream consequences. “No reception” is useful scope, not a positive label: off-ball influence could be present, absent, beneficial, neutral, or harmful.

## 12. Explainable tactical-analysis applications

A possible future path is:

**validated tracking primitives → validated football-concept signatures → tactical interpretation → automated candidate detection → explainable video overlays/examples → coaching, scouting, player-development, broadcast, or grassroots applications**

The desirable system would not merely output “PIN: 87%.” It would show how the block moved, how the focal defender differed, which attacker relationship persisted, and what spacing changed. No such system currently exists in this project.

## 13. Explicit nonclaims

This framework does not establish pinning, dragging, tracking, covering, handoffs, overload response, recovery, decoy influence, defensive response in a tactical sense, relational reconfiguration, attacker causation, defensive quality, gravity, or off-ball value. It defines a disciplined route for testing those ideas.
