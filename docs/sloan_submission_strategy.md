# Sloan submission strategy — September 2026

**Decision:** build a **hybrid measurement-and-validation paper** around a
replicated observational finding: attacker movement is associated with
spatially localized movement of nearby defenders relative to their defensive
unit. Do not frame the work as attacker value, gravity, causal influence, or
automatic tactical recognition.

This strategy uses only closed governed evidence. It introduces no scientific
classification and authorizes no new experiment.

## 1. The paper that exists today

### Strongest defensible contribution

Tracking research already measures team centroids, player-to-team geometry,
dyadic coordination, pressure, marking networks, and controlled space. This
project's contribution is therefore not the invention of relative geometry.
It is a governed combination: a transparent leave-one-out defensive-unit
reference; continuous attacker movement defined without defensive outcomes;
start-fixed proximity ranks that require no inferred marking assignment; prior
movement conditioning; development, held-out, and cross-provider tests; and
negative controls that keep geometric reorganization separate from tactical
meaning and value. Across both Metrica sample matches and seven IDSSE matches,
greater attacker movement in fixed two-second intervals was associated with a
stronger concurrent defender-relative movement coefficient among nearby than
middle-ranked defenders. The pooled IDSSE near-minus-middle estimate was
0.05115 m/m, 95% interval [0.04595, 0.05642]. Attacker-aligned directional
geometry was also externally supported across all seven IDSSE matches, while
the Metrica Game 2 directional replication remained mixed. The paper can claim
a reproducible way to characterize **where and in what geometric form** a
defensive unit reorganizes alongside attacker movement. It cannot claim why
the defenders moved or whether the movement helped the attack.

### Best framing

**Hybrid of A (measurement-method) and B (validation/application), with a
bounded empirical football finding.** The primitive itself has limited
measurement novelty because player-centroid and player-team coordination are
established. The paper becomes meaningful through the validation strategy,
the replicated rank-localized empirical pattern, the directional complement,
and unusually explicit falsification boundaries. A pure empirical-behaviour
paper would invite causal and tactical questions the evidence cannot answer; a
pure method paper would undersell the nine-match, two-environment result.

### Strongest “so what?”

Football analysts often assert that a run moved the defence, but raw tracking
cannot distinguish one defender's adjustment from a shared defensive shift.
This work supplies a reproducible, assignment-free description of that
difference and shows that its association with attacker movement is spatially
localized and directionally structured. That is useful as a measurement layer
for analyst review, future context studies, and later semantic validation—even
though it is not yet an evaluation of whether the movement was tactically
correct or valuable.

## 4. Novelty assessment

### Established methodology

- Team centroids, stretch, shape, and player-to-centroid distance.
- Player-team synchrony, relative phase, velocity correlation, and dyadic
  coordination.
- Defender-attacker networks, inferred assignments, pressure, pitch control,
  receiver availability, and space-creation models.
- Fixed tracking windows, proximity ranks, block resampling, and continuous
  trajectory descriptions.
- Expected-versus-observed defensive architectures in basketball and
  counterfactual/ghosting approaches in football.

### Distinctive combination

Among the reviewed literature, the differentiated element is the combination
of leave-one-out defending-outfield reference, accumulated focal-relative
movement, attacker-only continuous windows, rank-localized attacker association,
strictly prior context, frozen controls, held-out Metrica replication, IDSSE
external replication, and prospective interpretation boundaries before any
tactical or value claim.

### Where novelty actually sits

1. **Primary:** validation strategy and reproducible empirical finding.
2. **Secondary:** conceptual decomposition of defensive shift, movement within
   the unit, spatial localization, and directional form.
3. **Secondary:** defensive application of established geometric families.
4. **Limited:** metric novelty; the exact leave-one-out accumulated path is a
   refinement, not a new general family.

Do not claim invention of centroid-relative movement, player-team
decomposition, coordination analysis, proximity networks, pressure, pitch
control, defensive assignments, gravity, or off-ball value. Do not claim the
combination is universally unprecedented; say it was differentiated among the
reviewed literature.

## 8. Recommended empirical analyses (maximum two)

### Analysis 1 — external replication of the time-ordered bridge

**Question:** Does the frozen association between preceding attacker movement
and subsequent localized defender-relative movement transport to the seven
IDSSE matches at native cadence?

**Why it matters:** The strongest full temporal bridge and footprint are Final
A only within two Metrica sample matches. IDSSE currently validates concurrent
localization and directional form, not the complete preceding-to-subsequent
bridge. This is the clearest external-validity gap in the paper.

**Story-changing result:** Consistent positive local/nonlocal and
near/middle distinctions under the unchanged design would support an
externally replicated temporal bridge. A mixed result would narrow the paper
to concurrent geometry; a negative result would make the measurement paper
more appropriate than a response paper.

**Meaning of a null:** The defensive-relative primitive may transport while
the specific temporal bridge does not. That is interpretable and publishable
as a boundary.

**Data role:** IDSSE seven matches; no Metrica Game 3. Existing Metrica Games
1–2 remain development/held-out history. **Role:** confirmatory external
replication. **Expected Codex cost:** high.

### Analysis 2 — narrow contextual characterization

**Question:** Does the established near-versus-middle geometry differ under a
small, theory-fixed set of football contexts—preferably attacker movement
direction relative to goal/defensive unit and initial attacker position
relative to the defensive block—with ball relationship treated as context
rather than outcome?

**Why it matters:** It turns “nearby defenders move more differently” into a
bounded answer about when and where the footprint is stronger, without
claiming value. This directly improves coach, analyst, and reviewer relevance.

**Story-changing result:** A prospectively specified, replicated context
difference would support a context-sensitive defensive-footprint finding. It
would not create tactical classes. A null would show that the footprint is
more stable across the tested contexts than expected and would still simplify
the paper.

**Meaning of a null:** No evidence for moderation by the few tested contexts;
retain the overall replicated association and avoid subgroup stories.

**Data role:** Use already-open Metrica Games 1–2 and IDSSE only for development
and specification; reserve untouched Metrica Game 3 as the confirmatory
holdout for one frozen context hypothesis. If a single narrow hypothesis
cannot be frozen by mid-September, do not open Game 3. **Role:**
development-to-confirmatory. **Expected Codex cost:** medium-high.

Do not combine these into a large interaction search, discover context on Game
3, or add consequence outcomes.

## 11. Abstract concepts

### A — measurement-method emphasis

Football tracking records player motion but does not distinguish an individual
defender's movement from a shared defensive shift. We present a transparent
measurement framework that expresses each outfield defender relative to the
other defending outfield players, links that movement to outcome-independent
attacker trajectories, and localizes the association by start-of-window
attacker–defender proximity without inferring marking assignments. Protocols
were frozen before development, held-out, and external tests. Across Metrica
Sample Games 1–2 and seven matches from an independent IDSSE provider
environment, greater attacker movement was associated with stronger concurrent
defender-relative movement among nearby than middle-ranked defenders. The
pooled IDSSE near-minus-middle estimate was 0.05115 metres per attacker metre
(95% interval 0.04595–0.05642), and all seven match estimates were positive.
Attacker-aligned directional geometry was externally supported, although one
Metrica held-out directional interval was inconclusive. Synthetic and
prospective audits found no mechanical rank-scaling effect and substantially
reduced induced activity confounding, but cannot remove all concurrent common
causes. A separate opportunity test was negative. The contribution is a
replicated, interpretation-light measurement of localized defensive
reorganization—not evidence of causation, marking, tactical success, gravity,
or attacking value.

### B — empirical defensive-geometry emphasis

How does a defensive unit move around an attacker who is moving off the ball?
Using fixed two-second tracking windows, we separated each defender's motion
from the contemporaneous movement of the remaining defensive outfield players
and ordered defenders by their initial distance to the attacker. Across two
Metrica sample matches and seven externally sourced IDSSE matches, the
association between attacker path and defender-relative movement was
consistently stronger for nearby than middle-ranked defenders. In pooled IDSSE
data, the near-minus-middle difference was 0.05115 m/m (95% interval
0.04595–0.05642); all seven matches were positive. A complementary
attacker-direction projection showed D2–D3 exceeded D4–D7 in every IDSSE
match, with every interval above zero, though its Metrica held-out replication
was mixed. The rank profile was stepped rather than a smooth decay: far ranks
often rebounded, and all pooled rank coefficients were positive. Pre-interval
movement controls and synthetic audits reduce simple activity and rank
artifacts, but the analysis remains observational. A prospectively specified
teammate-separation consequence test was negative. The results establish a
reproducible spatial and directional signature of defensive geometry alongside
attacker movement, while withholding causal, tactical, and value claims.

### C — football/practitioner emphasis

Analysts say that an off-ball run “moved the defence,” but raw tracking cannot
tell whether a defender adjusted individually or simply travelled with the
whole unit. We developed a football-readable measure that subtracts the shared
movement of the other defending outfield players and asks where the remaining
movement is concentrated around a moving attacker. The method requires no
marking labels and was tested prospectively across Metrica Sample Games 1–2
and seven matches from an independent IDSSE tracking environment. Nearby
defenders showed a stronger attacker-movement association than middle-ranked
defenders in every governed match; the pooled IDSSE difference was 0.05115
metres per attacker metre (95% interval 0.04595–0.05642). Movement in the
attacker's direction also showed a reproducible nearby-defender pattern in all
seven IDSSE matches, although one Metrica held-out test remained uncertain.
The tool can describe and surface candidate moments for analyst review, but it
does not identify why defenders moved or whether the run was tactically useful.
Indeed, a separate test found no evidence that greater defensive reorganization
improved nearby teammates' defender separation. The result is a validated
measurement layer for studying how attacks and defensive units move together,
not an automated label or player-value score.

**Sloan appeal ranking:** A first, C second, B third. The final submission
should use C's opening hook with A's scientific spine. B is accurate but most
vulnerable to “defenders follow runners” and causality objections.

## 12. Lean paper/deck structure

1. Football problem: a run can coincide with defensive movement without
   receiving the ball.
2. Measurement problem: separate collective shift from movement within the
   defensive unit; distinguish geometry from interpretation.
3. Construct: leave-one-out defender-relative movement, attacker-only fixed
   windows, start-fixed proximity ranks, prior context.
4. Prospective evidence design: Metrica development and heldout replication,
   IDSSE external transport, controls and rank-composition audit.
5. Main result: nine-match concurrent localization and effect-size translation.
6. Spatial and directional form: stepped footprint, direction projection, and
   mixed Metrica directional evidence.
7. Football-readable measurement example and practitioner use.
8. Interpretation boundaries: common causes, negative opportunity result,
   mixed coverage and unsupported match-side identity.
9. Literature position: limited metric novelty; meaningful validation and
   application contribution.
10. Limitations and next evidence: external time-ordered bridge and one context
    hypothesis, not gravity or value.

Keep protocol history, segmentation failures, complete robustness tables, and
coverage-design lineage in supplementary material.
